The headline as typed: the same keys, pressed while the computer is set to the Russian layout. Nothing is lost; the converter below undoes it.

pilot exploratory results of 15 Nov 2026 and 16 Nov 2026

Keyboard-state input is text as typed, before or without conversion by an input method editor (IME). We tested 13 models in 21 settings (reasoning off or on) on four-option questions typed this way.

Ambiguous text: something is lost

pilot and follow-up, 16 Nov 2026 · exploratory · 18 same-spelling classes · one call per item, three in the follow-up · items not yet native-checked · follow-up plan fixed in advance

In each of 18 classes of same-spelling words, two questions send the model the same text, byte for byte, and need different answers. No reader can expect more than 50%.

Forced to pick an option, the 21 settings together score 48.9% [46.7, 50.6]: at the bound. Other same-spelling words, in sentences written to allow one reading, score 99.1% in the pilot, and 98.1% where the rarer word is the answer.

Where the text cannot settle the answer, the only better behaviour is to say so. In free text, 52.6% of calls do. When a closing line also asks for the option number, 21.6% do, and 60.6% of replies are a bare number.

A note saying that the text is pinyin without tones matters less than the closing line: 4.9 to 12.9 points over a control sentence that names neither, unevenly across these settings.

Free-text replies were labelled by model judges that are also models under test (claude-sonnet-5-5 and gpt-6.1-sol, with kimi-k3-1-pro-261024 on their splits); no person has checked a label.

Note: “Note: the Chinese text below was typed as Hanyu Pinyin without tone marks.” Control sentence: “Note: the Chinese text below was typed in lower-case letters with spaces between words.”

For input pipelines: three suggestions, not yet tested

Send the keyboard state (layout, input mode) with the text, where the client knows it. Naming the layout gave gpt-6.1-terra 28 to 30 points in two runs, and 6 other reasoning-off settings no clear gain.

Keep only same-spelling words in native script. In the earlier study this scored 1.00, but the kept words were the answers themselves.

Leave room to flag ambiguity. A reply that is a bare option number cannot flag it.

Method

Task

Each item is a reading-comprehension question from Belebele: a passage, a question and four options, in one of 122 language variants. The model answers with the number of one option. In a typed arm the passage, the question and the options all reach the model in the typed form (in the main run's question-only arm, only the question and options do).

Scoring

Answers are parsed strictly: only an answer that is exactly one of the four option labels counts. Every reply counts. A refused reply, a reply cut off at the output budget and a reply that does not parse count as wrong; this site shows them together as no valid answer and splits them in the detail view. Reasoning tokens are as each API reports them; cost per call is at list price.

Floor without reading

Floor without reading: expected accuracy of always picking the option with the largest share of its letter triples found in the passage, ties sharing credit, computed on the wrong-layout text: 34.8% on average, 29% to 41% by language. Chance: 25%.

It is the score of a rule that never reads the question, not a model's score without the passage; the main run will measure that separately (arm no passage).

Four steps

Hypothesis: reading wrong-layout text takes up to four steps. Models fail at different steps (a description, not a tested hypothesis).

  1. Recognise that a key map applies, and which: tested by naming the layout against a control sentence.
  2. Recall the map, or be given it: the real layout against random key maps that carry the same information, and a random map with and without its table.
  3. Apply it: a map given in the prompt against the text as written.
  4. Decipher it, where no map is known: random maps with no table, with reasoning on.

What the main run tests

Confirmatory: C1 to C5, tested together with a Holm correction (alpha 0.05). Four cross-script layouts under an English instruction for C1 to C4 (Russian, Hebrew, Armenian, Arabic); Mandarin for C5. Everything else is descriptive. The unit that is resampled is the Belebele passage for C1 to C4, the same-spelling class for C5. The plan, the items and the analysis code are frozen and time-stamped before the first main-run call. A public registration follows later.

The five tests and the items
  • C1 Full disclosure of the keyboard state. Accuracy with the layout named (b) minus accuracy with the control sentence (b'). Counted as supported only if it passes the Holm correction and (b) minus no sentence (a) is positive. Its two parts are secondary: recognition (b'') minus (b'), identification (b) minus (b'').
  • C2 The standard layout against random permutations of the same keys. Accuracy on the real wrong layout (a) minus accuracy on class-preserving random key maps of the same keys (d). A real layout may differ from a random one in more than exposure; the phonetic arm (c) is reported beside it.
  • C3 The information in the table. Random key map with its key table (f) minus the same map with a sham table of the same form and length (f0).
  • C4 The cost moves to tokens. Pairs of one model with reasoning off and on; the list of pairs is fixed at the freeze, after a protocol check on pilot items. In every pair the accuracy loss on wrong-layout text is smaller with reasoning on, and with reasoning on the model spends more reasoning tokens than on the text as written.
  • C5 Prompted abstention under the explicit format. Mandarin items whose typed text does not settle the answer, with a 'cannot tell' answer offered: share of replies that choose it after the disclosure sentence minus after the control sentence.

C1 to C4: Belebele items of draw ranks 50 to 149, the same for every setting (items whose passage also occurs among ranks 0 to 49 are left out); ranks 150 to 249 where the budget allows, as a secondary extended-set estimate. C5: the Mandarin items built at the bound. The pilots used ranks 0 to 49.

Exploratory, never part of a test
  • The roster: every registered setting that passed the 5-call smoke check, on fewer items and arms, for this explorer.
  • The map: all 122 Belebele variants as written and romanised or without diacritics, on five settings (gpt-6-luna effort none and high, deepseek-v4-1-flash thinking off, doubao-seed-2-1-lite thinking off, claude-haiku-5-5 thinking off).
  • Breadth: every language with a wrong-layout condition (29), and English typed on four other layouts.
  • The arm with another map's table (f x), on some settings only.
  • Japanese romaji and Vietnamese VNI keystrokes beside the Chinese and Vietnamese arms.
  • A secondary family on mechanism (spaced text, a generic hint, three examples, reversed options), never part of the five tests.
